Steam Frame Review

I recieved my Steam Frame roughly two weeks ago, and my first impressions have been very positive!



The Linux Experience

The thing I was most worried about was its compatability with Linux, as even though Valve is working on compatability with Linux PCs, they're not officially supported; Despite this I've had almost zero issues streaming from my Linux PC to the Steam Frame, with the exception of some connection issues the process has been near seamless. VR gaming on Linux has been, to be frank, pretty awful for quite a while now; While there were a plethora of ways to get it working and even have a quality experience, I found the experience always lagged behind that of Windows. This was especially true of SteamVR, it's one of the many reasons I stopped using my Valve Index after switching to Linux.The SteamVR experience wasn't great, but I couldn't figure out how to adjust the deadzone with SteamVR, so my choices were between VR that worked well or having controllers that didn't drift horribly. I'm happy to say that those days feel like they're in the past, my experience streaming games to the Steam Frame from my PC has been for the most part smooth sailing.



Standalone

The Steam Frame, despite being advertised primarily as PC streaming VR headset, can play vr games standalone directly on the headset, with some caveats that is. The Steam Frame's standalone performance legitiamtely impressed me, it's amazing what a tiny little ARM computer can do using FEX, I'd say roughly 25% of my VR library could run reasonably well standalone on the Steam Frame. That being said the battery life in standalone mode leaves much to be desired, not lasting more than an hour or two.

Visuals

At first I had a negative impression of the Steam Frame's display and lenses, and I went in expecting to be impressed, the Steam Frame's pancake lenses were on paper a major upgrade over the fresnel lenses in the Index, but it felt like a worse experience to me. However, I was using the glasses spacer, as I had not yet gotten my prescription inserts, and I knew that having the headset further away from your face and significantly impact FOV and the quality of the experience, so I waited. Once I was able to use the Steam Frame with prescription inserts it was a whole new experience, definitely an improvement over the Index.
